I have a weird standing for myself on tech. I went to an A grade technical college in the country, learned to code, and did courses that had a programming portion almost every other semester. I even work in a job which most people would call ‘techno’managerial. But I would never call myself a techie. If anyone does, it makes me feel like a serious fraud. Back in college, I couldn’t even complete my first programming lab correctly and I started taking help way earlier than other people in the same course. I could do a maximum of one out of three questions during the weekly assignment halfway into the course, and I would copy one more answer from Bhavesh. I would leave the third question untouched just so that there was less suspicion about me cheating. I scored 27/100 in my Data Structures midsem and dropped the course right after. It’s been quite a chest of failures when I look back at my programming history. 

At work, I’ve always thought I’ve been able to… wing it. Understand tech just enough to speak to my engineers and analysts. I try. But that’s all I do. Because I want to be smart for the next discussion, that’s it. This has made me feel like a fraud most times. Like I know something, but not enough. One more question and I would probably fall flat.

It’s weird, you know. You always feel like the cover is gonna come off of you. Some day, someone is gonna ask you one question and voila, it’s off.

But you know what? Some times, just some times, I feel it’s not a skill issue, it’s a confidence issue. There are probably enough PMs out in the world who navigated similar situations like I did, but without the self doubt. They would rather be proud of how little they needed to know to make a decision. 5 minutes, decision done. Maybe it’s even a superpower. I can see only what is needed. The details and complexities of engineering do not bother me, and they don’t have to. What if this is a strength? Maybe it really is. Only time will tell. Till then, I will continue telling myself that it doesn’t hurt to learn more, and possibly, finally, do something about it.
